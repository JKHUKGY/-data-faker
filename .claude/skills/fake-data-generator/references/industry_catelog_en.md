# North American Industry Classification (H1B Employment-Oriented Version)

This classification references GICS (Global Industry Classification Standard) and NAICS (North American Industry Classification System), streamlined for Chinese international students seeking H1B employment, excluding sectors with few positions or minimal sponsorship.

## 1. Technology

**H1B Share: ~55-60%**

The technology industry is the most H1B-friendly sector, accounting for over half of annual H1B approvals. High salaries and abundant positions, but cyclical layoff risks should be noted.

### 1.1 Software & Cloud

Enterprises and individuals purchase software products or subscribe to cloud services to solve business problems. This is the most mainstream track for international students, with high demand for SDE/DS positions.

#### 1.1.1 Enterprise Software

Software systems for enterprise operations management such as ERP, CRM, and HR systems. Customers are corporate IT departments, paying by license or subscription. Representatives: Salesforce, SAP, Oracle.

#### 1.1.2 SaaS Cloud Services

Software services delivered via the internet, users pay monthly/annually. Business model is "renting software" rather than "buying software", focusing on renewal rates. Representatives: ServiceNow, Workday, Atlassian.

#### 1.1.3 Cybersecurity

Protecting enterprises and individuals from cyber attacks and data breaches. Companies pay for compliance and loss prevention, considered "insurance-type" spending. Representatives: CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Fortinet.

#### 1.1.4 Data Infrastructure

Provides underlying tools for data storage, processing, and analysis. Main employment direction for data engineers and data architects. Representatives: Snowflake, Databricks, MongoDB.

### 1.2 Internet Platforms

Attracts users through free services, then monetizes through advertising or transaction fees. Users are the product, advertisers are customers. Traffic is king, winner takes all.

#### 1.2.1 Search & Advertising

Users search information for free, platform charges advertisers. Advertising revenue accounts for the majority of internet company revenue. Representatives: Google, Microsoft Bing.

#### 1.2.2 Social Media

Users socialize for free, platform sells targeted advertising. Data and algorithms are core competencies. Representatives: Meta, TikTok (ByteDance), Snap.

#### 1.2.3 E-commerce Platforms

Connects buyers and sellers, charges transaction commissions or advertising fees. Logistics and payments are moats. Representatives: Amazon, eBay, Shopify.

### 1.3 Hardware & Semiconductors

Designs and manufactures electronic devices and core chips. Capital intensive, high technical barriers, positions mostly for hardware engineers and chip designers.

#### 1.3.1 Semiconductor Design

Designs CPU, GPU, AI chips, selling to device manufacturers or cloud providers. Design relies on talent, manufacturing outsourced to TSMC. Representatives: NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm.

#### 1.3.2 Semiconductor Equipment

Manufactures equipment needed for chip production lines, selling to chip foundries. Concentrated customers (TSMC, Samsung), strong technical monopoly. Representatives: Applied Materials, ASML, Lam Research.

#### 1.3.3 Consumer Electronics

Designs and manufactures phones, computers, smart devices, selling directly to consumers. Brand and ecosystem are core. Representatives: Apple, Dell, HP.

### 1.4 IT Services & Consulting

Helps enterprises plan, implement, and maintain IT systems. Charges by project or headcount, essentially "selling person-days". High H1B sponsorship volume but lower salaries.

#### 1.4.1 Systems Integration

Helps enterprises integrate various software and hardware systems with custom development. Project-based, profit margins depend on project management capability. Representatives: Accenture, IBM, Deloitte.

#### 1.4.2 IT Outsourcing

Enterprises outsource IT operations and development to third parties. Indian companies dominate, fierce H1B quota competition. Representatives: TCS, Infosys, Wipro.

#### 1.4.3 Cloud Providers

Provides computing, storage, and networking infrastructure services, charging by usage. Strong economies of scale, three giants monopolize the market. Representatives: AWS, Azure, GCP.

## 2. Financial Services

**H1B Share: ~8-10%**

Financial industry overall has less H1B sponsorship, but quantitative, data, and risk control positions are exceptions. Good stability, competitive salaries, suitable for students with strong quantitative backgrounds.

### 2.1 Banking

Takes deposits, makes loans, earns interest spreads. Also provides payments, wealth management, and other services for fees. Heavily regulated industry, licenses are the biggest barrier.

#### 2.1.1 Commercial Banking

Provides loans, cash management, trade financing to enterprises. Earns corporate loan interest and service fees. Representatives: JPMorgan, Bank of America, Wells Fargo.

#### 2.1.2 Investment Banking

Helps companies with IPOs, bond issuance, M&A, collecting underwriting and advisory fees. Talent intensive, top salaries but high work intensity. Representatives: Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan.

#### 2.1.3 Retail Banking

Provides deposits, mortgages, credit cards to individuals. Acquires customers through branches and brand, undergoing digital transformation. Representatives: Chase, Bank of America, Citi.

### 2.2 Asset Management

Manages funds for clients (institutional or individual) and invests, collecting management fees and performance bonuses. Larger scale means lower costs, significant head effects.

#### 2.2.1 Mutual Funds & ETFs

Raises funds from the public to invest in stocks and bonds, collecting management fees (typically 0.1%-1%). Fee rates continue to decline with passive investing trend. Representatives: BlackRock, Vanguard, Fidelity.

#### 2.2.2 Hedge Funds

Raises funds from high-net-worth clients, using complex strategies to pursue absolute returns. Charges 2% management fee + 20% performance fee. Representatives: Bridgewater, Citadel, Two Sigma.

#### 2.2.3 Private Equity

Acquires non-public companies, improves operations, then sells for profit. Typically 5-7 year holding period, requires significant leverage. Representatives: Blackstone, KKR, Carlyle.

### 2.3 Insurance

Collects premiums, assumes risks, uses premiums to invest for returns. Actuarial pricing is core capability, strictly regulated, stable cash flows.

#### 2.3.1 Property & Casualty

Insures property losses and liability risks (auto, home, commercial insurance). Annual policies, pricing based on historical data and actuarial science. Representatives: State Farm, Travelers, Progressive.

#### 2.3.2 Life & Health

Insures death, illness, disability risks. Long policy terms, investment returns are important. Representatives: MetLife, Prudential, UnitedHealth.

#### 2.3.3 Reinsurance

Insurance for insurance companies, disperses catastrophic risks. Customers are insurance companies, highly globalized. Representatives: Munich Re, Swiss Re, Berkshire Hathaway Re.

### 2.4 FinTech

Uses technology to transform traditional financial services, reducing costs and improving experience. Regulation gradually tightening, pure cash-burning models are unsustainable.

#### 2.4.1 Payment Processing

Processes credit card, debit card, electronic payment transactions, charging fees per transaction. More transactions mean more profit, strong network effects. Representatives: Visa, Mastercard, PayPal.

#### 2.4.2 Digital Lending

Uses algorithms and data for credit assessment, issuing loans online. Faster than traditional banks but higher default risks. Representatives: SoFi, LendingClub, Affirm.

### 2.5 Securities & Trading

Provides securities trading channels and infrastructure, charging commissions or data fees. High-frequency trading and market makers need top technical talent.

#### 2.5.1 Brokerage

Provides stock, options trading channels for investors. Zero commission trend relies on order flow and interest income. Representatives: Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood.

#### 2.5.2 Exchanges & Market Making

Provides trading venues or acts as intermediary between buyers and sellers. Profits from volume and spreads, large technology investment. Representatives: NYSE, Nasdaq, Citadel Securities.

## 3. Healthcare

**H1B Share: ~6-8%**

Heavily regulated, counter-cyclical, rigid demand. H1B sponsorship concentrated in pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and health tech fields. Biostatistics, data science, and regulatory affairs positions are suitable for international students.

### 3.1 Pharmaceuticals & Biotech

Researches, produces, and sells drugs. Long R&D cycles (10+ years), large investments ($1B+), relies on patent protection to recover costs.

#### 3.1.1 Big Pharma

Has mature drug pipelines, profits from blockbuster drugs (annual sales $1B+). Driven by both R&D and sales. Representatives: Pfizer, Johnson & Johnson, Merck.

#### 3.1.2 Biotech

Focuses on innovative therapies (gene, cell, antibody), most companies unprofitable. Survives on financing and acquisitions. Representatives: Amgen, Gilead, Moderna.

#### 3.1.3 Generics & Specialty

Produces generic drugs after patents expire, or focuses on rare diseases. Lower profit margins but stable cash flows. Representatives: Teva, Viatris, Jazz Pharmaceuticals.

### 3.2 Medical Devices

Designs and manufactures diagnostic and therapeutic equipment, selling to hospitals and clinics. FDA approval is the barrier, clinical evidence is the selling point.

#### 3.2.1 Large Medical Equipment

MRI, CT, surgical robots and other large equipment. High unit price, long sales cycles, after-sales service is important revenue. Representatives: GE Healthcare, Siemens Healthineers, Philips.

#### 3.2.2 Implants & Consumables

Cardiac stents, artificial joints, surgical consumables. High repeat purchase rate, physician relationships are important. Representatives: Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific.

#### 3.2.3 Diagnostics

In vitro diagnostics (IVD) and imaging diagnostic equipment. Molecular diagnostics demand exploded post-pandemic. Representatives: Danaher, Roche Diagnostics, Thermo Fisher.

### 3.3 Healthcare Services

Provides diagnostic services or manages healthcare spending. Strong localization, difficult to scale.

#### 3.3.1 Hospitals & Clinics

Provides outpatient and inpatient medical services. Revenue from insurance reimbursement, costs mainly labor. Representatives: HCA, CommonSpirit, Kaiser Permanente.

#### 3.3.2 Health Insurance

Manages healthcare spending, collects premiums from employers and individuals. Cost control capability is core competency. Representatives: UnitedHealth, Anthem, Cigna.

#### 3.3.3 Health IT

Provides electronic health records, healthcare data analytics software. Policy-driven, interoperability is industry pain point. Representatives: Epic, Cerner (Oracle), Veeva.

## 4. Energy

**H1B Share: ~2-3%**

Traditional energy is stable but slow growing, new energy is growing fast but policy-dependent. Engineering and data analysis positions have H1B opportunities, locations mostly in Texas, Houston.

### 4.1 Traditional Energy

Extracts, processes, and sells oil and natural gas. Commodity nature, prices fluctuate with supply/demand and geopolitics.

#### 4.1.1 Exploration & Production

Finds and extracts oil. Capital intensive, oil prices determine profitability. Representatives: ExxonMobil, Chevron, ConocoPhillips.

#### 4.1.2 Midstream

Transports and stores oil and gas, charges tolls. Stable cash flows, similar to utilities. Representatives: Kinder Morgan, Enterprise Products, Williams Companies.

#### 4.1.3 Refining & Chemicals

Processes crude oil into gasoline and chemicals. Profits depend on "crack spread", highly cyclical. Representatives: Valero, Marathon Petroleum, Phillips 66.

### 4.2 Renewables & Clean Tech

Generates power using solar, wind, and other renewable resources. Heavily dependent on policy subsidies, long-term positive but short-term volatile.

#### 4.2.1 Solar

Produces photovoltaic modules or operates solar power plants. Module prices continue to decline, Chinese companies dominate. Representatives: First Solar, Enphase, SunPower.

#### 4.2.2 Wind

Builds and operates onshore and offshore wind farms. Large upfront investment, low operating costs. Representatives: NextEra, Vestas, GE Vernova.

#### 4.2.3 Energy Storage

Battery and other storage technologies, solving renewable energy intermittency. EV growth drives lithium battery cost reduction. Representatives: Tesla Energy, Fluence, Stem.

### 4.3 Utilities

Provides basic services like electricity and natural gas. Regional monopolies, regulated prices, stable but slow growth.

#### 4.3.1 Electric Utilities

Generates, transmits, and distributes electricity to residents and businesses. Regulation guarantees reasonable returns, strong recession resistance. Representatives: Duke Energy, Southern Company, Dominion.

#### 4.3.2 Multi-Utilities

Provides electricity, natural gas, and water services simultaneously. Highly regional, M&A is main growth method. Representatives: Sempra, Xcel Energy, National Grid.

## 5. Industrials

**H1B Share: ~3-4%**

Manufacturing and infrastructure-related industries. Highly cyclical, closely correlated with economic growth. Engineering positions have H1B opportunities, but overall sponsorship willingness is lower than tech.

### 5.1 Aerospace & Defense

Manufactures aircraft, spacecraft, and defense equipment. Customers are airlines and governments, long order cycles, high technical barriers.

#### 5.1.1 Commercial Aerospace

Manufactures commercial aircraft and engines. Duopoly (Boeing vs Airbus), complex supply chain. Representatives: Boeing, GE Aerospace, RTX.

#### 5.1.2 Defense Contractors

Provides weapons systems and military equipment to government. Government budgets determine revenue, politically sensitive. Representatives: Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, Raytheon. Note: Defense positions typically require citizenship, limited H1B opportunities.

#### 5.1.3 Space & Satellites

Launches rockets, operates satellites, provides space services. SpaceX disrupted traditional pricing, commercial space is booming. Representatives: SpaceX, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman.

### 5.2 Transportation

Moves people and goods. Infrastructure-heavy, labor intensive, generally low profit margins.

#### 5.2.1 Airlines

Provides passenger and cargo air services. Fuel costs and load factors are key. Representatives: Delta, United, American.

#### 5.2.2 Railroads

Most economical method for long-haul freight. Oligopoly, strong pricing power. Representatives: Union Pacific, BNSF (Berkshire), CSX.

#### 5.2.3 Logistics & Delivery

Provides package delivery and supply chain services. E-commerce drives growth, last-mile costs are high. Representatives: UPS, FedEx, XPO.

### 5.3 Industrial Manufacturing

Produces machinery, electrical products, and industrial materials. B2B nature, customers are other enterprises.

#### 5.3.1 Machinery

Produces machinery for factories, construction, and agriculture. Correlated with capital expenditure cycles, after-sales service accounts for significant revenue. Representatives: Caterpillar, Deere, Illinois Tool Works.

#### 5.3.2 Electrical Equipment

Produces electrical components, automation equipment, and power systems. Industrial automation and electrification are growth engines. Representatives: Emerson, Rockwell Automation, Eaton.

## 6. Retail & Consumer

**H1B Share: ~3-4%**

Consumer-facing industries. Fierce competition, low profit margins, H1B sponsorship opportunities concentrated in headquarters functional positions (data, supply chain, finance).

### 6.1 Retail

Sells goods to consumers, earns buy-sell margins. Online-offline integration, efficiency and experience are key.

#### 6.1.1 General Retail

Large retailers selling multiple product categories. Walmart relies on low prices, Costco relies on membership fees. Representatives: Walmart, Costco, Target.

#### 6.1.2 E-commerce Retail

Online product sales, wins through logistics and algorithms. Amazon dominates, profits come from AWS and advertising. Representatives: Amazon, eBay, Chewy.

#### 6.1.3 Specialty Retail

Focuses on specific categories (home, electronics, beauty). Strong vertical expertise, high customer stickiness. Representatives: Home Depot, Best Buy, Ulta.

### 6.2 Consumer Products

Produces and sells products for personal use. Brand and distribution are moats, consumer upgrading and health trends are prominent.

#### 6.2.1 Food & Beverage

Produces packaged food, beverages, and alcohol. High consumption frequency, strong brand loyalty. Representatives: PepsiCo, Coca-Cola, Nestlé.

#### 6.2.2 Household & Personal Care

Produces daily chemicals, cleaning, and personal care products. Essential consumer goods, strong recession resistance. Representatives: Procter & Gamble, Unilever, Colgate.

#### 6.2.3 Apparel & Footwear

Designs and sells clothing and footwear. Strong fashion attributes, inventory management is challenging. Representatives: Nike, Lululemon, VF Corporation.

### 6.3 Restaurants & Leisure

Provides dining, accommodation, and entertainment services. Labor intensive, economically sensitive.

#### 6.3.1 Restaurant Chains

Operates restaurants or franchise systems, collects meal fees or franchise fees. Standardization and supply chain are core. Representatives: McDonald's, Starbucks, Chipotle.

#### 6.3.2 Hotels

Provides accommodation services, or operates hotel brands collecting management fees. Asset-light model (brand licensing) is preferred. Representatives: Marriott, Hilton, Airbnb.

## 7. Communication & Media

**H1B Share: ~2-3%**

Provides communication services and content. Telecom has utility characteristics, media entertainment is content competition.

### 7.1 Telecommunications

Provides voice, data, and network connectivity services. Infrastructure-heavy, oligopoly, slow growth but stable cash flows.

#### 7.1.1 Wireless Carriers

Provides mobile communication and data services. User numbers peaked, relies on price increases and value-added services. Representatives: Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile.

#### 7.1.2 Telecom Equipment

Manufactures network infrastructure (base stations, routers, fiber optics). 5G investment cycle, Western manufacturers benefit from Huawei restrictions. Representatives: Cisco, Ericsson, Nokia.

### 7.2 Media & Entertainment

Creates and distributes content, attracts audiences, sells advertising or subscriptions. Streaming disrupted traditional models, content is king.

#### 7.2.1 Streaming

Provides video and music content by monthly subscription. User growth slowing, profitability becoming focus. Representatives: Netflix, Disney+, Spotify.

#### 7.2.2 Gaming

Develops and sells video games. Games as a Service (GaaS) and in-app purchases are mainstream models. Representatives: Microsoft (Xbox), Sony, Electronic Arts.

#### 7.2.3 Traditional Media

Television, film, publishing. Advertising revenue declining, transitioning to digital. Representatives: Comcast, Warner Bros Discovery, News Corp.

## 8. Professional Services

**H1B Share: ~5-7%**

Industries providing intellectual services. Talent is the core asset, charges by project or hour. Consulting companies have more H1B sponsorship, but competition is fierce.

### 8.1 Management Consulting

Helps enterprises solve strategy, operations, and organizational problems. Sells thinking frameworks and person-days, high brand premium.

#### 8.1.1 Strategy Consulting

Provides strategic advice to CEO level. MBB three firms monopolize high-end market, top salaries but high attrition. Representatives: McKinsey, BCG, Bain.

#### 8.1.2 General Consulting

Provides comprehensive services from strategy to implementation. Large scale, broad coverage, technology consulting growing fast. Representatives: Deloitte, Accenture, PwC.

### 8.2 Accounting & Tax

Provides audit, tax, and financial advisory services. Heavily regulated, license requirements, Big Four monopolize large client market.

#### 8.2.1 Audit & Assurance

Audits financial statements, issues independent opinions. Public companies must be audited, price competition is fierce. Representatives: Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG.

#### 8.2.2 Tax Services

Helps companies and individuals plan taxes and file returns. Complex tax law creates demand, highly seasonal. Representatives: Big Four, H&R Block, Grant Thornton.

### 8.3 HR Services

Helps enterprises recruit, manage, and pay employee compensation. HR outsourcing and flexible employment are growth points.

#### 8.3.1 Staffing & Recruiting

Provides temporary workers, contractors, or executive search services. Economically sensitive, technical talent is most profitable. Representatives: Robert Half, Korn Ferry, ManpowerGroup.

#### 8.3.2 Payroll & HR Software

Provides payroll, benefits management, and HR software. Subscription model, high stickiness. Representatives: ADP, Paychex, Workday.

## 9. Real Estate

**H1B Share: ~1-2%**

Develops, operates, and invests in real property. Capital intensive, regional, interest rate sensitive. Data analysis and asset management positions have H1B opportunities.

### 9.1 Real Estate Development

Develops and operates properties. Earns rent or sale profits, leverage is common.

#### 9.1.1 Commercial Real Estate

Develops and operates office buildings, shopping centers, and hotels. Remote work impacts office, retail transitioning to experience. Representatives: CBRE, JLL, Brookfield.

#### 9.1.2 Residential Real Estate

Builds and sells homes or operates apartments. Interest rate sensitive, population migration affects regional demand. Representatives: D.R. Horton, Lennar, AvalonBay.

#### 9.1.3 Industrial Real Estate

Builds and operates warehouses, logistics centers, and data centers. E-commerce drives warehouse demand, data centers are new hotspot. Representatives: Prologis, Equinix, Digital Realty.

### 9.2 Real Estate Services

Provides property transaction, valuation, and management services. Asset-light model, relies on professional capability and client relationships.

#### 9.2.1 Real Estate Brokerage

Represents property sales or leasing, charges commission. Residential market is fragmented, commercial market is concentrated. Representatives: Realogy, Compass, Marcus & Millichap.

#### 9.2.2 Property Management

Manages property daily operations on behalf of owners. Stable income, limited economies of scale. Representatives: CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield, Greystar.

## 10. Materials

**H1B Share: ~1-2%**

Produces basic industrial materials. Commodity nature, prices fluctuate with supply and demand. Chemical and specialty materials positions are friendly to STEM backgrounds.

### 10.1 Chemicals

Produces chemicals, supplying to various industries. Capital intensive, strong economies of scale, environmental pressure.

#### 10.1.1 Commodity Chemicals

Produces ethylene, polymers, and other commodity chemicals. Prices fluctuate with oil prices and supply/demand. Representatives: Dow, LyondellBasell, BASF.

#### 10.1.2 Specialty Chemicals

Produces high-performance, customized chemicals. High technical content, good profit margins. Representatives: DuPont, PPG, Sherwin-Williams.

#### 10.1.3 Agricultural Chemicals

Produces fertilizers, pesticides, and seeds. Food security drives demand, highly seasonal. Representatives: Corteva, Nutrien, FMC.

### 10.2 Metals & Mining

Extracts and processes metal minerals. Extremely cyclical, tied to global economy and infrastructure investment.

#### 10.2.1 Steel

Produces steel for construction, automotive, and manufacturing. Overcapacity is a long-term issue, China accounts for half of global production. Representatives: Nucor, Cleveland-Cliffs, Steel Dynamics.

#### 10.2.2 Industrial Metals

Extracts copper, aluminum, lithium, and other industrial metals. Electrification and new energy drive copper and lithium demand. Representatives: Freeport-McMoRan, Alcoa, Albemarle.

## 11. Education

**H1B Share: <1%**

Provides education and training services. Traditional education has few H1B opportunities, EdTech has some opportunities but industry cooling down.

### 11.1 EdTech

Uses technology to provide online education and training. Post-pandemic growth slowed, profit models still being explored.

#### 11.1.1 Online Learning Platforms

Provides online courses for individuals or enterprises. B2C difficult to profit, B2B corporate training more stable. Representatives: Coursera, Udemy, LinkedIn Learning.

#### 11.1.2 Education Software

Provides teaching management software for schools. Government budgets determine spending, long sales cycles. Representatives: PowerSchool, Instructure, Blackboard.
